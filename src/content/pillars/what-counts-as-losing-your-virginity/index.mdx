Virginity isn't a physical thing you can measure. There's no test a doctor can do, and nothing about your body proves whether you've had sex or not. The old idea that the hymen "breaks" the first time is a myth. The hymen is just a thin bit of tissue that's shaped differently in different people, and it can stretch or wear away from all sorts of everyday things.

So "losing your virginity" is really an idea, and people mean slightly different things by it. Most use it to describe the first time someone has penetrative sex with a partner. But some count other kinds of sex, some don't count anything until it feels emotionally significant, and plenty of people think the whole idea is more trouble than it's worth.

None of those are wrong. There's no official rulebook, which is why the same question keeps coming up: *does this count?* The honest answer is usually that it counts if it counts to you.

What matters far more than the label is whether sex is something you want, whether everyone involved has agreed to it, and whether you're looking after yourself and each other. Those things are real. "Virginity" is just a word people use to talk about them.
